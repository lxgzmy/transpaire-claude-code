How to raise a build contract with Claude

Give Claude the build contract request email and it fills in the contract documents for you — using Transpire’s own templates off the Z: drive, not something it made up. You check it before anything is saved or sent.

Everyday guide · Contract Administration

It uses the real template, every time. Claude opens the same blank document you would open from Z:\PROCEDURES & FORMS\CONTRACTS and types into it. The wording, the styles, the inclusions list and the page numbering are left exactly as they are — only the fields change. It has been taught which template belongs to which region (Tamworth and Gunnedah jobs share one; Sydney and SEQ have their own), which folders are superseded, and the nine fields on page 1 plus the client name and address blocks on pages 10–13, from the Raising Contracts section of the System and Procedures Manual. It will never invent a contract format. If it can’t find the right template it stops and tells you.

Start here — five steps

  • 1
    Open Claude Desktop and sign in with your own account.
  • 2
    Switch to Code mode.
  • 3
    Pick the project folder transpire-claude-code.
  • 4
    Accept the folder‑trust prompt. Once only.
  • 5
    Type /new-contract-template and point it at the request email.

The folder to pick in step 3 is Z:\CLAUDE CODE\transpire-claude-code — browse to it once and Claude remembers it.
Steps 1–4 are a one-off; after that you land straight on step 5 each time. For the email, either save it out of Outlook as a .msg file and give Claude the path, or just paste the subject line — it can find the message itself.

What happens when you run it

1

It reads the whole email chain, and the attachments

Including the EOI, even when it is a photo of a signed form.

What it pulls out
  • The buyer’s name, from the EOI — not from an email signature
  • Design, façade and contract price
  • Whether the EOI is marked dual key
  • Anything the sales manager asked for — a review first, no prelim agreement, a changed price

Why this matters: these emails get forwarded two or three times. The instruction to you is in the newest part, the job facts are in the oldest part from the marketer. It reads all of it.

Requests to you are repeated back, not acted on. If the email says something should be reviewed or sent, Claude tells you it says that and leaves the decision with you.

2

It picks the template and tells you which one

And why it chose that one, before it fills anything in.

You get back: the full path of the blank template it is about to use. Check it — this is the most useful thing on the screen.

It knows what is out of date. Anything in an SS folder is superseded, and there are a lot of them — a dozen old NSW contract files that look perfectly current. It also skips files marked “don’t use” and any template named for a different marketer or estate. If two templates could both be right, it asks rather than guessing.

3

It fills in the inclusions

Page 1, and the client name and address blocks on pages 10 to 13.

The fields it completes
  • Lot number, street, suburb, estate and price
  • House type, house size, façade and garage side
  • Client name in every place it appears, and the full site address
  • Builders Representative

You get back: a finished .docx, plus a list of exactly what changed against the blank template — so you can see it touched the fields and nothing else. A preliminary agreement too, if the job needs one.

Signatures, dates and initials are left empty. Always. Those are yours and Michael’s to sign.

4

It tells you what it could not fill

This is the part worth reading properly.

Typical flags
  • “Garage side isn’t in the email or the plans — it needs confirming”
  • “The plans haven’t arrived, so house size is blank”
  • “This job already has a contract from June — is this an amendment?”
  • “The ID attachments don’t match the name on the EOI”

You get back: a short review sheet listing every field, where each value came from, and every flag. Nothing is saved into the job folder until you say yes.

What it will never do

  • Never invent contract wording, or a contract format. It fills the company’s template or it stops.
  • Never guess a number that goes in a contract. Price, house size, garage side and the prelim fee are either sourced or flagged for you.
  • Never sign, initial or date anything, and never send an email or issue anything to DocuSign.
  • Never save into a job folder without showing you first, and never overwrite a file that is already there.

Saying “yes” to one thing does not approve the next one. It asks every time.

What it produces, and what it doesn’t
DocumentWhat you get
Inclusions Filled in, ready to check. The real Word template with all the fields completed.
Preliminary agreement Filled in, when the job needs one. The fee is never carried over from the template — it asks, because the template figure has been wrong in practice.
Build contract A list of values, not the document. The NSW and QLD contracts are locked PDFs with no fillable boxes, so nothing can type into them. Claude gives you every value you need — cover page, price, building period, progress payments — and you key it in.
Plans, general conditions, colour options Not produced. Listed as a checklist so nothing is forgotten from the pack.

Everything it hands you is a draft. Read it against the email and the plans before it goes anywhere. It is quick and it is careful, but it has not seen the site, spoken to the client, or opened DataBuild.

If something goes wrong
ProblemFix
“I can’t see the Z: drive” The task is running in the cloud. Start again in Code mode on your computer.
It says the template has changed Take this seriously. Somebody has revised the blank template, so Claude stopped rather than produce a half-filled contract. Tell the team — it needs a small update before it will run again.
It left garage side blank Working as intended. It is read off the site plan drawing and Claude will not guess it. Fill it in yourself, or ask Michael.
It won’t produce the build contract It can’t — the contract is a locked PDF. Use the list of values it gives you.
It says the price on the email and the EOI disagree It stops on purpose. Don’t pick one — get it confirmed.
It won’t change the inclusions for an auxiliary dwelling Also on purpose. Which inclusions apply to a second dwelling is a pricing decision, so it goes to Michael rather than being written by Claude.
The job isn’t where you expected Finished and cancelled jobs move out of the live region folder. Claude checks those too — give it the job number.
