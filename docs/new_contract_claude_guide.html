How to raise a build contract with Claude

Give Claude the build contract request email and it sets the job up in OnSite Companion, then fills in the contract documents for you, using Transpire’s own templates off the Z: drive, not something it made up. It saves the finished documents into the job folder and hands you a review sheet. Every OnSite Companion change waits for your Yes, and nothing is ever sent, signed or issued without you.

Everyday guide · Contract Administration · updated 11 September 2026

It uses the real template, every time. Claude opens the same blank document you would open from Z:\PROCEDURES & FORMS\CONTRACTS and types into it. The wording, the styles, the inclusions list and the page numbering are left exactly as they are. Only the fields change. It has been taught which template belongs to which region (Tamworth and Gunnedah jobs share one; Sydney and SEQ have their own), which folders are superseded, and which fields to fill, from the Raising Contracts section of the System and Procedures Manual. It will never invent a contract format. If it can’t find the right template it stops and tells you.

Start here: five steps

  • 1
    Open Claude Desktop and sign in with your own account.
  • 2
    Switch to Code mode.
  • 3
    Pick the project folder transpire-claude-code.
  • 4
    Accept the folder‑trust prompt. Once only.
  • 5
    Type /new-contract-template and point it at the request email.

The folder to pick in step 3 is Z:\CLAUDE CODE\transpire-claude-code. Browse to it once and Claude remembers it.
Steps 1 to 4 are a one-off; after that you land straight on step 5 each time. For the email, just paste its subject line from Outlook: Claude finds the message and its attachments in the mailbox itself. (A saved .msg file also works, if the email is not in the mailbox.) Start a new chat for each job so it picks up the latest instructions.

Where the documents go: four rules

  1. It looks in the job folder first. Before filling anything, Claude checks the job’s CONTRACT DOCUMENTATION folder under Z:\PROJECTS and notes which contract documents are already there: inclusions, preliminary agreement, build contract.
  2. A document the job does not have yet is a real contract. Its Word file and PDF are saved straight into that CONTRACT DOCUMENTATION folder. It never goes to the testing folder.
  3. A document the job already has is treated as a re-run. The new copy goes only to the testing folder (Z:\CLAUDE CODE\cowork-projects\3.new_contract\template-testing\<job>). The original in the job folder is never overwritten. If the new one is right, you move the old one into the folder’s SS subfolder and copy the new one across.
  4. The build contract is produced too, every time. Until the manager files the approved Word contract in the templates folder, Claude fills from the team’s own Word build and saves it under the real file name. The report tells you which template it used; a person reads the draft against the licensed HIA PDF before it is issued.

This is decided document by document. On a job that already has its inclusions but no build contract yet, one run puts the inclusions re-run in the testing folder and the build contract straight into the job folder. The run report prints a line per document saying where it went.

What happens when you run it

1

It reads the whole email chain, and the attachments

Including the EOI and the ID, when Claude can read them.

What it pulls out
  • The buyer’s name, from the EOI, not from an email signature
  • Design, façade and contract price
  • Whether the EOI is marked dual key
  • Anything the sales manager asked for: a review first, no prelim agreement, a changed price

Why this matters: these emails get forwarded two or three times. The instruction to you is in the newest part, the job facts are in the oldest part from the marketer. It reads all of it.

Scanned EOIs and IDs. This server has no OCR, so a scanned or photographed EOI or ID often cannot be read. When Claude cannot read it, it asks you for the names. Type them exactly as they appear on the ID, including middle names, and check the spelling on the finished document yourself.

2

It sets the job up in OnSite Companion

New from 11 September 2026. Each change is shown to you first and waits for your Yes.

What it does in OSC
  • Searches the lot first, so an existing job is never created twice
  • Creates the client and the job: region, generated contract number, the Pre Sales Investor v1 template
  • Fills the job details from the EOI: site address, design and façade, property type, local council, legal and marketer details
  • Ticks activities 1 and 2, and attaches the request email to the job and to task 11 with the subject NEW JOB
  • Adds the purchasers as contacts with mobile and email, ticks them as primary, and adds the sales and marketer contacts

You get back: each OSC change before it is sent and a read-back after it, plus the job’s contract number, which becomes the job number for the folder and the documents.

DataBuild is still yours. Claude never opens it, and never puts the contract price into OSC (the price lives in DataBuild only). Enter the job in DataBuild as you do now, then tell Claude “DataBuild done” and it ticks activity 6, Create Job within Databuild. Anything OSC’s API has no field for, such as Client Notes, is listed for you to type in.

3

It finds the job folder and picks the template

And tells you both, before it fills anything in.

You get back: the job folder it found on Z: (it also checks the completed and cancelled folders, and warns you if a cancelled job sits at the same lot), the full path of the blank template it is about to use, and a line per document saying whether that document will go to the job folder or the testing folder. If the job folder does not exist yet, Claude asks you before creating it.

It knows what is out of date. Anything in an SS folder is superseded, and there are a lot of them. It also skips files marked “don’t use” and any template named for a different marketer or estate. If two templates could both be right, it asks rather than guessing.

4

It fills the documents and saves them

Inclusions, the preliminary agreement if the job needs one, and the build contract.

The fields it completes
  • Inclusions: lot, street, suburb, estate, price, house type, house size, façade, garage side, client names, site address, Builders Representative
  • Build contract: cover page (owners, job, lot, site), the owner block, the land details, the signature names and the owner acknowledgement names
  • Owner address, mobiles, emails, DP number and land address, when the request gives them

You get back: each document as a finished .docx and its PDF, saved per the four rules above, plus a list of exactly what changed against the blank template so you can see it touched the fields and nothing else.

Signatures, dates, initials and money are left empty. Always. Signing is yours and Michael’s. Price excluding GST, GST, contract price and deposit come from DataBuild and are typed only when you give Claude the exact figures. The progress payment schedule is never filled by Claude.

5

It tells you what it could not fill

This is the part worth reading properly.

Typical flags
  • “Garage side isn’t in the email or the plans, it needs confirming”
  • “The plans haven’t arrived, so house size is blank”
  • “This job already has inclusions, so the new inclusions went to the testing folder; the build contract went into the job folder”
  • “Owner names were typed in by you, not read from the ID. Check the spelling”
  • “Dual key: the standard inclusions have no second-dwelling section. Someone needs to write it”

You get back: a short review sheet listing every field, where each value came from, every blank, and every flag. It also lists the values you still need to key in yourself (see the checks below).

Before anything is issued: your checks

  1. Read the build contract against the licensed HIA PDF once. The Word template is the team’s own build and is still waiting on the manager’s formal sign-off, so this read-through is your safety check on layout and wording.
  2. Check the owner names and spelling against the ID. If Claude could not read the EOI, the names came from what was typed into the chat.
  3. Key the money from DataBuild. Contract price, GST, deposit and the progress payment schedule are never filled automatically.
  4. Finish the OSC job. Do the DataBuild entry yourself and tell Claude it is done, so activity 6 gets ticked. Type in any Client Notes the report lists.
  5. Fill what is still blank. Owner address, every purchaser’s mobile and email (DocuSign needs all of them), DP number, land address and any guarantor details stay empty until someone supplies them.
  6. Dual key and plans. The auxiliary dwelling inclusions text has to be written by a person; house size and garage side wait for the plans; confirm the building period category.
  7. Check the report’s route lines so you know which documents are in the job folder and which are in the testing folder waiting to be moved across. Claude never issues, sends or signs anything.

What it will never do

  • Never invent contract wording of its own, or a contract format. It fills the company’s template or it stops. The one thing it writes per job is the Sydney UPGRADED INCLUSIONS section, and there the words come from the Standard Variation spreadsheet; it shows you the row it picked and how sure it is, and anything it could not match is marked for your approval.
  • Never guess a number that goes in a contract. Price, house size and garage side are either sourced or flagged for you. The preliminary agreement fee is the standard amount unless the request names another.
  • Never sign, initial or date anything, and never send an email or issue anything to DocuSign.
  • Never change OnSite Companion without showing you the change first and getting your Yes, and never open DataBuild.
  • Never overwrite a file that is already in a job folder. A document the job already has is re-run into the testing folder for you to check and move across; only documents the job does not have yet are saved straight in.

It still stops and asks when a real decision is needed: a price that doesn’t match the EOI, two templates that could both fit, whether the job needs a preliminary agreement, or whether to create a job folder that does not exist yet.

What it produces, and what it doesn’t
DocumentWhat you get
Inclusions Filled in, ready to check. The real Word template with all the fields completed. Sydney (from 10 September 2026): the job’s content too. Section 18 keeps only the area that applies (Box Hill and Gables, North Kellyville, or everywhere else), the red Bathroom 2 and air-conditioning notes are dealt with, the upgrades from the request are written into UPGRADED INCLUSIONS in the Standard Variation wording, and the whole document is black. It then re-levels the two columns in Word and checks its own work (labels level, headings at the top of their pages, no blank page, nothing red or highlighted) before it will save. The report lists every upgrade line with the spreadsheet row it used and a confidence mark; check the medium and low ones.
Preliminary agreement Filled in, when the job needs one. Claude asks whether the job needs it. The standard fee is used unless the request names a different one.
Build contract Filled in and saved into the job folder. Claude fills the cover page, the owner block, the land details and the signature names on the team’s Word version of the licensed NSW or QLD contract, and exports the PDF (45 pages for NSW, 37 for QLD). The report names the template it used. Prices, GST, deposit and progress payments always stay yours; they come from DataBuild. Read it against the licensed HIA PDF before it is issued.
Plans, general conditions, colour options Not produced. Listed as a checklist so nothing is forgotten from the pack.

Everything it hands you is a draft. Read it against the email, the ID and the plans before it goes anywhere. It is quick and it is careful, but it has not seen the site, spoken to the client, or opened DataBuild.
